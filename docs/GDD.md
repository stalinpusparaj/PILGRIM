# PILGRIM: THE NARROW WAY — GAME DESIGN SPEC (GDD)

> Single source of truth. If code and this document disagree, this document wins. If it is silent, choose the simplest option that serves the Design Pillars and log it in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## 2.1 Identity

- **Title:** PILGRIM: THE NARROW WAY
- **Credit line (title screen, store page, credits):** Based on John Bunyan's *The Pilgrim's Progress* (1678)
- **Pitch:** A little pilgrim with a big burden walks the narrow way to the Celestial City.
- **Genre:** Third-person 3D story adventure with gentle platforming, puzzles, mini-games and one boss.
- **Audience:** Kids 6-12 and families; also churches, Sunday schools and homeschool groups.
- **Rating target:** ESRB E (Everyone) / PEGI 7. Cartoon peril only.
- **Platform:** Desktop and mobile browsers (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox, Android Chrome, iOS Safari 16+). Must run well on a budget Android phone.
- **Length:** 13 chapters + epilogue, 2.5-3.5 hours. Chapters 8-22 minutes.
- **Languages:** English and Tamil at launch (text only; voices are language-free babble, so localisation is cheap).
- **Build flags:** `VITE_DEMO=true` (Chapters I-IV only), `IGNORANCE_EPILOGUE=false` (default), `VITE_STORE_URL`.

## 2.2 Design pillars

1. **Feel the allegory.** Every spiritual state is a mechanic a child can feel, not a lecture.
2. **The way is narrow.** The right path always glows a little. Detours always look fun and always cost something.
3. **Toy-box charm.** Chunky, bouncy, colourful, funny. A laugh in every chapter.
4. **Grace, not grind.** No XP, no shops, no loot. Gifts arrive at story moments.
5. **Made here, by us.** Every model, texture, sound, tune, font choice and icon is generated by our own code or drawn by us. Zero third-party assets (see 2.3).

## 2.3 Asset policy (hard rule, enforced by a script)

- **Allowed:** open-source **code** libraries and dev tooling. Runtime dependency allowlist: `three` only. Code modules from `three/examples/jsm` (e.g. `BufferGeometryUtils`, `TransformControls`) are allowed because they are code. Dev dependencies: `vite`, `typescript`, `@playwright/test`, `eslint`, `prettier`.
- **Not allowed:** any model, texture, HDRI, image, sprite, icon set, sound, music, voice, font file, animation (no Mixamo), shader pack or sample asset made by anyone else, including assets bundled in `three/examples`. No AI-generated assets from outside services.
- **How we make everything:** geometry, colours, animation, terrain, sky, particles, music, SFX and voices are generated at runtime by code in `src/gen/` and `src/audio/`. Icons and the logo emblem are hand-written inline SVG in our repo. Text uses **system fonts** (nothing downloaded).
- **Enforcement:** `tools/check-assets.mjs` fails the build if `public/` contains any binary file other than our own generated favicon/PWA icons (also generated by our code at build time), or if `package.json` dependencies contain anything outside the allowlist.
- **Bonus:** with no asset downloads, the whole game should be **under 3 MB** gzipped and start in seconds on Indian 4G. Treat this as a selling point.

## 2.4 Core loop

- **Second to second:** walk the Way, look around, bump into things, talk, pick up, jump.
- **Minute to minute:** meet someone, choose what to say, solve a small trial (a puzzle, a mini-game, a traversal, a temptation), reach a waystone where the Roll updates.
- **Chapter to chapter:** a new land, new colours, a new tune, a new toy-like mechanic, a set piece, a chapter card.

## 2.5 Camera

- Third-person, 4.5 m behind and 2.2 m above, looking slightly down (toy-diorama feel). Spring arm with collision against simple colliders.
- **Auto-camera** (default on for touch, optional on desktop): the camera gently swings behind the player while walking, so kids never have to manage it.
- Burdened: camera 0.2 m lower and a slow sway (can be disabled with "Reduce camera motion").
- Cutscenes: spline rails, letterbox bars, gentle eased moves. No shaky cam.
- FOV 55 default, slider 45-75.

## 2.7 Player systems

### 2.7.1 The Burden
- A big lumpy sack tied with rope, almost as tall as Christian's torso and head. Comic and readable from behind.
- Burdened: walk 2.0 m/s, no running, 0.3 m hop, leaning forward 20 degrees, slow heavy stride, the sack bounces (spring physics on its pivot), a "huff" puff of breath every few steps.
- Reading the Book (Ch I) makes it grow with a cartoon "boing" and a groan in the music.
- On the Wrong Hill detour (Ch III) it grows again and Christian's feet start to drag ruts in the ground.
- **The Cross (Ch VI):** input slows, music fades to silence, the ropes snap one by one (3 plucked SFX), the sack tumbles down the hill, bounces twice and drops into the open tomb with an echoing "thoom." 1.5 s of silence. Then the main theme. Christian straightens up with a big stretch. Free: walk 3.4 m/s, run 6 m/s, jump 1.1 m. The first three jumps after this are special "joy jumps" with sparkles and rising notes. Give 20 safe seconds of meadow to run around before anything else happens.

### 2.7.2 Hope (the lantern)
- 0-100, shown as a small lantern on the HUD whose flame grows and shrinks. No numbers.
- Drains in sad and dark places (Slough, Valley of the Shadow, Doubting Castle, River) at a rate set per zone, and when Christian walks alone in the Slough (after Pliable leaves) and the Valley of the Shadow.
- Restored by companions' cheering lines (automatic when Hope is low and a friend is near), reading a Promise in the Satchel (+15, once per Promise per chapter), praying (+20), waystones (sets at least 60).
- Low Hope (<30): colours desaturate, music thins, companions say kind things.
- At 0: screen fades, then **I SHALL ARISE** and you wake at the last waystone with Hope 60.

### 2.7.3 Bonks (no deaths)
- Falling into a bog hole, a ditch, a pit or getting hit by an arrow = cartoon "bonk": a puff of dust, stars over the head, and Christian pops back at the last safe spot on the Way (not the waystone). Hope -5. Text: "Oops! Back on the Way."

### 2.7.4 The Roll (save, journal, ticket)
- Given at the Cross. Before that, Evangelist's note is the only page.
- **Waystones** along the Way: "[E] Read the Roll" saves, raises Hope, shows "The Roll is updated."
- **Arbour (Ch VII):** sitting makes Christian nap (Zzz bubbles), the Roll slips under the bench. Waystones show "You need the Roll" until it is found. If the player never sits, achievement "Wide Awake."
- **Celestial Gate:** the last action of the game is "[E] Show the Roll."

### 2.7.5 Promises (collectibles)
- 24 glowing paper scrolls with short King James lines (public domain), 1-3 per chapter, often slightly off the path or behind a small challenge.
- Read in Satchel > PROMISES for Hope.
- **Slough:** stepping stones glow up when Christian stands still for 2 s or reads a Promise.
- **River:** more Promises collected = faster Hope recovery while praying.

## 2.8 Companions and dialogue

### Companions (Pliable, Faithful, Hopeful)
- Follow 2-3 m behind-left, match speed, hop over small gaps with the player, teleport behind the player out of view if more than 25 m away.
- Pathing: simple steering along the Way spline plus local avoidance (in-house, no navmesh library). Off the Way they follow the player's breadcrumb trail.
- **Barks:** short speech bubbles on triggers (low Hope, Promise nearby, detour entered, idle 20 s). Max one bark per 20 s.
- Look at the player when talking, look at points of interest marked in level data.
- Faithful catches Christian after the pride trip (Ch X). Hopeful holds him up in the River (Ch XIII).

### Dialogue system (in-house)
- Dialogue lives in `content/dialogue/chXX.json` as a small graph format:
  ```json
  { "id": "ch01_evangelist", "nodes": [
    { "id": "n1", "speaker": "evangelist", "text": "ch01.evangelist.1", "anim": "point_far", "next": "n2" },
    { "id": "n2", "speaker": "christian", "text": "ch01.christian.1",
      "choices": [ { "text": "ch01.choice.run", "next": "n3", "effects": { "hope": 5 } },
                   { "text": "ch01.choice.ask", "next": "n4" } ] } ] }
  ```
  Effects supported: `hope`, `give`, `take`, `detour`, `flag`, `anim`, `cam`, `sfx`, `music`, `end`.
- All text lives in `content/i18n/en.json` and `ta.json` by key.
- **World-space speech bubbles** over heads for barks and simple lines; a bottom panel with portrait icon for conversations with choices (max 3 choices).
- **Babble voices** (2.12) play while text appears at 40 characters per second.
- **Writing rules for kids:** reading age 8. Max 2 short sentences per bubble. Max 5 bubbles before the player acts or chooses. Keep Bunyan's best short lines when kids can follow them; otherwise write fresh, simple, warm lines. Every named character's name explains them; show that in Satchel > FRIENDS MET ("Pliable: says yes to everything, gives up fast.").
- Never use any line from the film. Never paste the film transcript into the project.

## 2.9 Combat (Chapter VIII boss + Chapter IX shadows only)

### Apollyon (Chapter VIII)
- **Design (original, from Bunyan's description):** a huge upright beast with fish-like scales made of big faceted plates, wide leathery wings, heavy bear-like paws, a lion-like mane and jaw, and a furnace glow in his belly that brightens before he breathes smoke. Cartoon-majestic, not gory. Big readable shapes, glowing tells.
- **Arena:** a long valley floor (40 x 18 m) with a few rocks for cover. Sky darkens with each phase.
- **Before the fight:** Apollyon claims Christian as his subject (Bunyan's idea). 3 short choices, all of them saying no in different ways (brave, polite, funny).
- **Phase 1 - Paws:** big telegraphed paw slams (paw glows red for 0.8 s). Block with the shield, then his belly-glow opens for 2 s: hit it with the sword. 3 hits.
- **Phase 2 - Fiery darts:** he flies up and throws slow, big glowing darts in volleys of 3. Perfect blocks bounce them back; 3 bounced hits knock him down for a 4 s sword window. 3 hits.
- **Phase 3 - The fall:** he grabs and throws Christian down; the sword spins away and lands 4 m off (slow motion). Prompt: "[E] Reach for the sword" (5 s; no limit if "Timed prompts" is off). Christian says Bunyan's Micah 7:8 line ("when I fall, I shall arise") and strikes. Apollyon flaps away in a sulk of smoke.
- **After:** a hand of light drops glowing leaves that heal all hearts. Calm music. Chapter card.
- Difficulty: **Easy** (default for kids: double telegraph time, half damage, bigger perfect-block window) and **Normal**.

### Shadows (Chapter IX)
- Smoky blobs with two ember eyes. They never hurt health; they drain Hope when close and nudge Christian toward the path edge. Prayer makes them flee squeaking. Sword scatters them for 3 s. They can never be destroyed.

## 2.10 Chapters (level design)

Each chapter is a **procedurally built world** from a level file (2.14), not a downloaded scene. "Film ref" is a structure reference only (see 1.4).

### CHAPTER I - THE CITY OF DESTRUCTION (10-12 min) - film ref 0:00-20:00
- **World:** a crooked, crowded little walled town at dusk. Smoke curls from chimneys, houses lean on each other, grey-orange sky, a gate in the town wall, open fields outside. Far away on the horizon: one tiny shining light.
- **Beats:** Christian wakes at home; the Book glows on the table: "[E] Read." The burden boings bigger. His wife Christiana and the four boys worry (2 short scenes: day, then evening). Walk through town: neighbours shrug, laugh, or ignore him (silly reactions). Outside the gate, Evangelist meets him, gives a note ("Flee from the wrath to come") and points to the far light, which becomes the objective. The family calls from the door; Christian covers his ears and runs (Bunyan's moment; short and gentle). Obstinate and Pliable chase him. Obstinate stomps home; Pliable bounces along, asking about golden streets.
- **Teaches:** move, look, talk, read, choose, the Burden.
- **Palette:** dusk orange #E8A26B, plum sky #6B4A5E, stone #8C8577, timber #6E5D50, horizon light #FFE8A3.

### CHAPTER II - THE SLOUGH OF DESPOND (8-10 min) - film ref 20:00-25:05
- **World:** a wide, gloopy bog under drizzle, bubbling mud, croaking frogs, crooked reeds.
- **Beats:** Pliable chatters about crowns. Both sink. Sinking mechanic: running in the mud sinks you faster, standing still for 2 s reveals glowing stepping stones under the mud. Pliable flails, gets out on the home side and stomps off in a huff. Alone, the lantern dims. Help, a big friendly man, reaches down: "[E] Take his hand." He shows the steps that were there all along.
- **Laugh:** Pliable losing a boot to the mud with a loud slurp.
- **Palette:** mud #5B4A35, reeds #8A8B5A, sky #9AA3A0, steps glow #F5E6B8.

### CHAPTER III - THE WRONG HILL (10-12 min) - film ref 25:05-34:30
- **World:** rolling green hills. A smooth sunny side road to a pretty village under a gigantic leaning mountain. The true Way: narrow and a bit bumpy.
- **Beats:** Mr. Worldly Wiseman, very fancy, very sure of himself, tells Christian to go see Mr. Legality in the village of Morality. The side road is lovely at first. Under the mountain the sky darkens, flashes of fire flicker (slow, under 3 per second), pebbles rattle down, the burden grows, Christian's feet drag. The village door never seems to get closer. Evangelist appears, explains kindly, and leads Christian back to the Way; Hope comes back as they rejoin it.
- **Laugh:** Wiseman's hat keeps getting blown off by the mountain wind and he pretends it didn't.
- **Palette:** hills #8DBF6A, sky #A9D3E8, mountain #5E4B5C, fire flashes #FF8A3D.

### CHAPTER IV - THE WICKET GATE (8-10 min) - film ref 34:30-40:00
- **World:** a high wall with a small wooden gate; on a nearby ridge, a gloomy castle. Dark arrows fall from it.
- **Beats:** arrows come in volleys with a whistle and a red line on the ground 1 s before landing. Christian (still burdened, no running) moves from rock to rock between volleys; a hit = bonk back to the last rock. At the gate: "[E] Knock." Goodwill yanks him inside just as a volley lands where he stood. Goodwill points him down the straight Way to the Interpreter's House.
- **Demo end** here if `VITE_DEMO=true`.
- **Palette:** wall #C9BBA0, gate #8A5A3B, castle #3E3548, safe light #FFF2C2.

### CHAPTER V - THE INTERPRETER'S HOUSE (15-18 min) - film ref 37:30-46:50
- **World:** a warm, candle-lit house. The Interpreter, holding a candle, shows six rooms. Each room is a 2-3 minute toy-puzzle with a short lesson, and **each one is a hint for a later chapter** (set-up/pay-off).

| Room | Puzzle | Lesson | Pays off in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The Portrait | Turn 3 lamps to light a painting of a wise, kind guide holding a book | A true guide looks up and points the way | Ch XII (spotting the Flatterer) |
| 2. The Dusty Room | Sweeping makes a dust storm that hides the room; sprinkling water from a jug settles it | Trying harder doesn't clean a heart; grace does | Ch VI (the Cross) |
| 3. Passion and Patience | Two kids: one gets a pile of toys now (they crumble), one waits. Pick who to sit beside | Wait for the better gift | Ch X (Vanity Fair) |
| 4. Fire and Oil | Someone throws water but the fire grows; walk round the wall to find someone secretly pouring oil | Help you cannot see keeps you going | Ch IX (the Valley) |
| 5. The Brave Man | Guards block a palace door; a man asks the scribe to write his name and charges through. Player can give their own name to the scribe | Be brave and go in | Ch VIII (Apollyon), and your name appears on your Roll at the end |
| 6. The Iron Cage | A sad man in a cage. You cannot open it. Lantern dims here | Despair is a cage; remember this | Ch XI (Doubting Castle) |

- **Palette:** candle #F2C98A, wood #8B5A3C, warm red #B5523B.

### CHAPTER VI - THE CROSS (10-12 min) - film ref 46:50-50:40
- **World:** a narrow road with low walls on both sides climbs a green hill at dawn. A plain wooden cross on top. Below it, an open stone tomb.
- **Beats:** slow, heavy climb. The Burden scene (2.7.1). Three Shining Ones (tall, glowing, kind; no wings, no halos): "your sins are forgiven", new white clothes with gold trim (a sparkle-dissolve outfit swap), a little glowing mark on the forehead, and the Roll. The Key of Promise is quietly added to Satchel > CARRIED (no message). Joy jumps and free running in the meadow. Further on: Simple, Sloth and Presumption snoring in chains (wake-them attempts get funny sleepy replies). Formalist and Hypocrisy tumble over the wall instead of using the gate and tag along.
- **Palette:** dawn #F7C9A8 to #FFE9A8, grass #9ED26A, cross #A0703F, tomb #B9B3A6.

### CHAPTER VII - HILL DIFFICULTY AND PALACE BEAUTIFUL (18-22 min) - film ref 50:40-58:40
- **World:** a spring at the foot of a steep hill; three paths (the Way straight up, Danger left into a dark wood, Destruction right into fog). An arbour halfway. Two lions at the top in the dusk. A gentle palace beyond.
- **Beats:** drink at the spring. Formalist and Hypocrisy take the side paths (later heard calling, lost). Climb: switchbacks, ledges ("[Space] Climb"). The arbour (2.7.4). Timorous and Mistrust run past downhill yelling about lions. At the top: lions roar; Watchful the porter calls "Keep to the middle of the path!" The chains only show within 6 m; step off the centre line and a lion lunges to the end of its chain (big scare, no harm). Palace: Watchful and the four sisters of the house (Discretion, Prudence, Piety, Charity) welcome him; short chats, each gives a Promise; Christian talks about missing his family. Sleep in the room called Peace (sunrise wake). The armoury: gifts one by one (2.7.6), outfit swap to armour. Courtyard practice: sword on straw targets, shield against soft training darts, perfect block, prayer. From the roof, a look at blue mountains far away (Ch XII).
- **Palette:** hill green #7FBF5F, dusk #8C7BC4, palace #F4EEE2 with blue roofs #6C8FB3.

### CHAPTER VIII - THE VALLEY OF HUMILIATION (10-14 min) - film ref 58:40-1:05:30
- **World:** a quiet green valley that turns purple and smoky.
- **Beats:** the sisters walk him down, then he goes on alone. Apollyon lands. Boss fight (2.9). Healing leaves. Chapter card.
- **Palette:** valley #7FAF6A to sky #5B4A7A, fire #FF7A2F, Apollyon teal scales #3F6F78 and wine-red wings #8C2F39.

### CHAPTER IX - THE VALLEY OF THE SHADOW (10-12 min) - not in the film
- **World:** very dark. A narrow path, a ditch on the right, a bog on the left, little red embers in the distance. Halfway: a smoking pit that blocks the path. Second half at dawn: snares and pits now easy to see.
- **Beats:** two men running back warn him off. The lantern is the only light (radius shrinks as Hope falls). Shadows (2.9). Whispers appear as italic thought bubbles ("Turn back...", "You're too small for this...") so they feel like Christian's own thoughts; kid-safe and gentle. At the smoking pit, only Prayer pushes the smoke back. A voice ahead recites Psalm 23:4 (KJV): someone else is on the road. Dawn breaks; walk the trap section carefully.
- **Palette:** dark #14121C, lantern #FFD27A, embers #E2553A, dawn #9FB8D9.

### CHAPTER X - FAITHFUL AND VANITY FAIR (18-22 min) - film ref 1:05:30-1:19:30
- **World:** open road, then the walled town of Vanity: one long street of striped stalls, flags, jugglers, music, and crowds.
- **Beats:**
  1. Faithful walks ahead. Running to pass him makes Christian grin proudly and trip on his face; Faithful helps him up (lesson about pride, played for a laugh).
  2. Talkative joins and cannot stop talking (his bubbles overlap and pile up comically). Pick the questions that show he only talks; he wanders off.
  3. Evangelist warns them that the town ahead will be hard and one of them will go to the King first.
  4. The fair: stall-keepers pop up offering games, sweets, hats and trinkets ("[E] Look" / "Yes please" / "No thank you"). "Yes please" adds a small sack to your back (2.7.8). The crowd notices the pilgrims' strange clothes and talk. When asked what they will buy, Christian answers with Bunyan's line "We buy the truth."
  5. Arrest; the pilgrims sit in a cage in the square while people stare (some kind faces in the crowd).
  6. The trial: Lord Hate-good in a huge wig, witnesses Envy, Superstition and Pickthank, and a jury of twelve whose name cards pop up as the camera passes (kid-safe names, see 1.5). Christian gets 2 choices to speak; the verdict doesn't change.
  7. Faithful goes ahead: the verdict is read, then night. A chariot of light comes down for Faithful and carries him up. The crowd falls silent. No execution shown or described.
  8. Night: a short sneaking section through the empty fair to the gate, avoiding lantern-carrying watchmen (caught = back to the start of the section). At the gate, Hopeful (a young man of the fair moved by Faithful) joins.
- **Palette:** pink #E64B8B, mustard #F2C230, violet #7B4FD6, teal #2EC4B6, night #22304A.

### CHAPTER XI - BY-PATH MEADOW AND DOUBTING CASTLE (18-22 min) - film ref 1:19:30-1:30:10
- **World:** the plain of Ease, a little hill with a sparkly silver mine, a pillar of salt, a clear river with fruit trees and lilies, a rocky stretch of road with a stile into a soft meadow, a storm, and a grey castle.
- **Beats:**
  1. By-ends and his friends (Mr. Hold-the-world, Mr. Money-love, Mr. Save-all) walk along for a bit, agreeing with everyone. They go down to the silver mine and are not seen again.
  2. Demas waves from the mine (optional detour).
  3. The pillar of salt with "Remember Lot's wife" (+1 Promise).
  4. River rest: eat fruit, Hope full.
  5. Rocky road; Christian suggests the soft meadow over the stile; Hopeful is unsure; the player must cross (story). Vain-confidence strolls ahead with a lantern and falls into a pit in the dark (cut before he lands; just a "whoooa" and silence).
  6. Storm and flood: no way back. They shelter and fall asleep.
  7. Giant Despair (very tall, wide, droopy, slow) finds them and carries them off under his arms (comic, not scary).
  8. Dungeon: dark, the lantern dims. Christian's lines get gloomy; Hopeful answers each with something kind. The giant stomps down twice, booms "You'll never get out!" and stomps up. His wife Diffidence (tall, thin, pointy, bossy) whispers bad advice to him through the floorboards (Bunyan has her advise him at night; our own original jokes).
  9. **Key puzzle:** the player must open Satchel > CARRIED and use the Key of Promise on the door. No prompt. After 90 s, Hopeful says something like "Didn't you get something at the Cross?" After 180 s, Christian remembers by himself. Remember the Interpreter's iron cage: this time the door opens. Three locks: cell, outer door, iron gate (stiff: hold E for 3 s while it grinds).
  10. Despair wakes, chases, then freezes mid-stomp in the morning sunshine (Bunyan: he has fits in sunny weather), comic.
  11. At the stile, "[E] Put up a warning sign" for future pilgrims.
- **Palette:** meadow #A6D96A, storm #39414F, castle #6F6A73, key gold #FFC94A.

### CHAPTER XII - THE DELECTABLE MOUNTAINS AND THE ENCHANTED GROUND (15-18 min) - film ref 1:30:10-1:34:10
- **World:** beautiful mountains with orchards, vineyards and lots of sheep. Then a fork in the road, then a warm, hazy, sleepy land.
- **Beats:**
  1. Four shepherds (Knowledge, Experience, Watchful, Sincere) welcome them. Optional: help round up 5 runaway sheep (kids love this).
  2. Hill Clear viewpoint: the looking glass. Wobbly hands (hold Pray to steady); a glimpse of the Celestial Gate for 2 s.
  3. Shepherds give two warnings: beware the Flatterer; do not sleep on the Enchanted Ground.
  4. Ignorance, a cheerful young man who came by a crooked little lane, says he'll get in just fine; he walks his own way.
  5. The fork: a smiling man in a white robe with a shiny mirror mask says he knows the way. Remember the Portrait room: a true guide points up and to the Way; this one points only at you and flatters. ## 2.11 Art direction (STYLE LOCK)

> **Low-poly toy diorama.** Flat-shaded faceted geometry with vertex colours only (no image textures). Chunky, rounded, friendly shapes. Characters are about 2.5 heads tall with big round heads, dot eyes that blink, simple swappable mouths, expressive eyebrows, mitten hands and stubby legs. Soft warm lighting from one sun plus a sky/ground hemisphere light. Gentle gradient skies, soft fog, puffy low-poly clouds. Bright but harmonious colours chosen from each chapter's palette. Everything should look like it could be a painted wooden toy. All original designs; no likeness to any film, show or game character.

- **Shading:** `MeshLambertMaterial` or `MeshToonMaterial` with `flatShading: true` and `vertexColors: true`. One shared material per material type to keep draw calls low. Optional soft rim light in a custom shader on High quality only.
- **Shadows:** one directional shadow map (1024 desktop, off on Low) plus cheap blob shadows under every character on all qualities.
- **Post-processing:** none required. On High only: subtle bloom on emissive objects (in-house small bloom pass or `UnrealBloomPass` from three/examples), FXAA. Default relies on MSAA (`antialias: true`).
- **The Way:** a slightly brighter, warm, smooth ribbon of path with little edge stones, always readable from the camera. Detours use a duller, cooler colour that looks just as inviting from a distance.
- **Skin tones** (spread across heroes and villains equally): #F1C7A3, #E0A57A, #C68A5E, #9C6644, #6E4A33.
- **Expressions:** neutral, happy, sad, scared, surprised, angry, sleepy, determined. Built from eyebrow angle, eye scale/blink, mouth shape swap.

## 2.12 Procedural asset system (`src/gen/`)

### Core utilities
- Seeded RNG (mulberry32 or similar, written in-house) and in-house 2D/3D simplex or value noise. Every world is generated from a seed, so it looks the same every time.
- `Palette` objects per chapter; all colours come from the palette.
- Geometry helpers: low-poly sphere, capsule, beveled box, cone, cylinder, lathe, extrude, "jitter" (randomly offset vertices for hand-made feel), vertex-colour painting, merge (BufferGeometryUtils), instancing helpers.

### CharacterFactory
- Builds a character from a **recipe** (JSON): body type, proportions, skin tone, clothing colours, hair/beard/hat parts, props, expression set.
- Parts: torso (beveled box or lathe), head (low-poly sphere), eyes, brows, mouth set, arms (upper/lower), mitten hands, legs, feet, hair/beard/hat variants (cap, hood, tall hat, wig, bun, curls, beard), props (staff, candle, lantern, keys, book, gavel, crook, sword, shield, sack).
- **Rigid-skinned rig:** each part's vertices are weighted 100% to its bone (hips, spine, chest, neck, head, shoulders, elbows, hands, hips L/R, knees, feet, plus prop and sack bones). All parts are merged into **one SkinnedMesh per character** (1 draw call) with a generated `Skeleton`. Face parts (eyes, brows, mouths) are separate tiny meshes on the head bone so expressions can swap.
- **Procedural animation library** (`src/gen/anim/`), driven by code curves, not keyframe files: idle (breathing), walk, run, burdened walk, hop, joy jump, climb, push, sit, nap, wake, kneel, pray, read, knock, sweep, reach, pulled up, trip and get up, talk (head bob + mouth flap synced to babble), wave, point, cover ears and run, shiver, laugh, cry, sword swing, shield raise, shield block, hit react, thrown down, reach for sword, wade, swim struggle, supported walk, carried under arm, snore, jury nod, crowd cheer/jeer. All blendable with a small in-house blend system.
- Squash and stretch on jumps and landings; secondary bounce on hats, hair and the Burden.
- **Crowds:** `InstancedMesh` per crowd body type with per-instance colour and a vertex-shader bob/sway animation. 1 draw call per body type.
- **Apollyon, giants, lions, sheep:** special recipes on the same system (giants are scaled humans with heavier proportions; Apollyon has extra wing and tail bones).

### World builder
- **Terrain:** heightfield per chapter (e.g. 300 x 300 m, 128 x 128 grid) from seeded noise plus hand-placed "shape stamps" (hill, valley, plateau, bog basin, cliff). Flat-shaded, vertex-coloured by height, slope and zone.
- **The Way:** a Catmull-Rom spline from the level file. Terrain is flattened along it, path colour painted, edge stones placed. Detours are side splines. The Way spline also drives companion pathing and the objective light.
- **Prop builders:** trees (cone, round, dead, fruit), bushes, reeds, grass tufts (instanced), rocks, fences, walls, stiles, timber houses, stalls, flags, cages, gate, cross, tomb, palace, castle parts, dungeon, armour rack, bridge-less river banks, lilies, flowers, vineyards, pillar of salt, mine pit, arbour, waystones, lamps and candles.
- **Scatter:** rules per zone (type, density, min distance, slope limit, keep-clear distance from the Way).
- **Water and mud:** in-house shaders (vertex wave, faceted normals, colour by depth; mud bubbles as animated instanced spheres).
- **Sky:** gradient dome shader with sun disc and low-poly instanced clouds. Fog colour from palette.
- **Particles:** in-house instanced particle system for dust, sparkles, smoke, embers, rain, Zzz, fire, light motes.
- **Generation must not freeze the page:** build heavy geometry in chunks across frames or in a Web Worker, behind the loading screen. Cache built geometry per chapter in memory while the chapter is loaded.

### Character Lab (dev tool)
- `?lab=1` opens a turntable scene listing every character recipe, every animation and every expression, with sliders to tweak colours and proportions and a "Copy recipe JSON" button. Use it to review the cast before building chapters.

## 2.13 Audio (all in-house, `src/audio/`)

- **Engine:** Web Audio API. Buses: Master, Music, Voice, Effects, Ambience. Dialogue ducks music by 6 dB. Unlock audio on the first tap or click.
- **Synth:** in-house instruments built from oscillators, noise buffers, envelopes, filters and a generated reverb impulse: soft pluck, warm pad, bell, flute-like lead, bass, marimba, soft drums, choir-like "aah" (stacked detuned oscillators with formant filters).
- **Music:** in-house step sequencer. Songs are note data in `content/music/*.ts`, written as **original melodies only** (never transcribe any existing song, hymn or film score). Themes: Pilgrim theme (main, hopeful), Burden motif (slow, low, 3 heavy notes), Wiseman waltz (charming, slightly silly), Interpreter music box, Apollyon march (low and heavy), Valley drone, Vanity Fair jig (fast, a bit too much), Despair drone, Mountains pastoral, Celestial finale (full, bright, bells). Music crossfades by state: calm, tension, sad, boss, joy.
- **SFX recipes** (parameter objects, generated on load): footsteps per surface (dirt, stone, mud, wood, grass, water), burden creak and huff, burden boing, rope snaps x3, sack roll and tomb "thoom", Roll unroll, Promise pickup chime, knock, arrow whistle and thunk, bonk and stars, lion roar and chain rattle, sword whoosh and hit, shield clang and perfect-block ring, dart whoosh, prayer swell, shadow squeak, cage clank, gate lock grind, net drop, thunder and rain, river rush, bells, crowd murmur, frog croaks, sheep baa, snores.
- **Babble voices:** each character has pitch, speed, waveform and vowel filter settings. One short syllable blip per 2-3 letters of text. Sad lines pitch down, excited lines pitch up.
- **Ambience** per zone, positional where useful (town bells, bog frogs, candle crackle, wind, fair crowd, river, birdsong).

## 2.15 Technical architecture

### Stack
- **Vite + TypeScript (strict)**, static site, no backend.
- **Runtime dependency: `three` only** (exact version pinned). WebGLRenderer, `antialias: true`, sRGB output, ACES or AgX tone mapping with gentle exposure.

- **Storage:** IndexedDB via a tiny in-house wrapper for saves; `localStorage` for settings (wrapped in try/catch).

## 1.4 BANNED LIST - film inventions you must not use

Ideas in general cannot be owned, but these specific characters, names, scenes and lines are the film's own creative expression. Using them in a commercial game invites a claim. Do not use:

1. The city's backstory and narration, its old and new names, the founder figure, and the ruling official class and their numbering.
2. The villain officials, their bumbling assistant, and every scene of them reporting to their dark master.
3. The earlier pilgrim who vanished first and whose papers are burned, the search for him, and his map.
4. Legality portrayed as a talking mountain with signs to follow.
5. Flying creature swarms attacking at the Wicket Gate, and the man fighting them off.
6. The Interpreter's motto about looking and listening, his light-following entrance, and the forked sign choice after his house.
7. The burden loosening step by step while a hymn plays.
8. Watchful as father of the four sisters.
9. Apollyon disguised as a prince using the family's voices.
10. The comic trial dialogue, the "hardship" plan, the rock-throwing scene, and the lazy-guard escape.
11. Hopeful as a former guard from Christian's city.
12. The giant's wife's comic insults and pet names, the nightgown gag, and the coat-of-arms deduction in the dungeon.
13. One shepherd who turns out to be a divine figure, his bleeding at the river, and Apollyon attacking in the river.
14. Hopeful crossing first, and the reunion-and-letter epilogue.
15. The film's renamed places: use Bunyan's names (**Slough of Despond, Doubting Castle, Delectable Mountains**), not the film's.
16. Any line of the film's dialogue, its score, its song recordings, character designs, colours, or the way any shot is framed.

## 1.5 What to learn from the film (craft, not content)

1. **Stakes need a face.** The film gives the enemy a voice between chapters. The game does the same with **Apollyon's own claim** from Bunyan ("this country is mine") shown as brief, original interludes: a shadow over the map, a cold wind, an empty throne on a far hill, never a cast of comic henchmen.
2. **Humour is structural.** Every chapter needs at least one laugh: Pliable's greed, the snoring sleepers in chains, Talkative who cannot stop, the fair's ridiculous stalls, the jurors' silly names (Bunyan's own, such as Mr. No-good, Mr. Blind-man, Mr. High-mind and Mr. Heady; swap Love-lust and Live-loose for kid-safe names like Mr. Greedy-guts and Mr. Lazybones), a giant who freezes mid-stomp in sunshine.
3. **Set up, then pay off.** The film plants the iron-cage man and pays it off in the dungeon. The game does this for every Interpreter room (each one is a hint for a later chapter, see 2.9 Ch V).
4. **Family is the emotional thread.** Christian leaves his wife and four boys. Bunyan's Part II is their journey. The game closes with Bunyan's Part II opening, which is also your **sequel hook**.
5. **Music carries the peak.** Silence before the burden falls, then the main theme. Original melody, written in code.

## 1.6 Kid-audience changes (things in the film or book that do not go in this game)

- No urging of suicide, no whispers about dying, no weapons offered to the prisoners. Despair says only "You'll never get out" type lines, and Hopeful answers each one.
- No threats of blood, no bleeding in the river, no stoning or beating shown.
- **Faithful's martyrdom:** the court gives its verdict; that night a chariot of light comes for Faithful and carries him up while the crowd stares. No execution is shown or described. Christian grieves. Kids understand "he went to the King first."
- Apollyon is big and fierce but readable and fair, never gory.
- The door to the bad place at the Delectable Mountains is cut. Ignorance's epilogue is **off by default** in the kids' build (flag `IGNORANCE_EPILOGUE`), because it ends on a frightening note. You decide.
- Cut the sectarian content (giants "Pope" and "Pagan", the "Rome" row) and redesign the Flatterer with a mirror mask, as in v1.
- **Skin tone never signals good or evil.** Heroes and villains are spread across all tones.
